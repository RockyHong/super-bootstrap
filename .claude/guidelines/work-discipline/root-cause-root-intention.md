# Root Cause and Root Intention — Grounding's Two Forms

A symptom arrives and the cheapest available move is to make that symptom go
away. Grounding is the refusal of that move, and it reaches the work moment
in two forms that are one principle: dig under what you were handed before
changing anything. The forms are told apart by **where the signal came
from** — never by what the signal looks like — and each names its own root.

- **Signal from the human — chase the root cause.** Feedback reports where
  the work broke down for them; it is a symptom, not the specification of the
  repair. The wording may happen to name a good fix and still be a reading
  rather than a remedy. Take it as evidence, trace it to the mechanism that
  produced it, and change the mechanism. Work that implements the words and
  leaves the mechanism standing returns the same feedback later under
  different words. Stop at the first mechanism that explains the signal and
  that this task can change; a cause past that boundary is recorded as an
  interim root, not chased here.
- **Signal from the system — chase the root intention.** An exception in
  front of you is the same kind of symptom: a carve-out already in the code,
  a special case the current state carries, one an agent or a plan merely
  proposes. Read it as a sign the general rule was stated too narrowly to
  cover a case that belongs inside it. Ask what general condition the
  exception stands in for, then widen the rule to say that condition — the
  exception disappears into the rule rather than sitting beside it. A widening
  that reaches past what was asked is itself a scope call: surface the widened
  rule against the request before making it.
- **A carve-out earns its place, or the general rule holds.** This is the
  exception's own root, and it binds the carve-out you are about to write as
  well as the one you found. It earns its place when the condition it names
  sits genuinely outside the rule's scope, and it then states that condition
  in the rule's own terms so the next reader can check it. Unearned, the
  general rule covers the case and the carve-out does not get written.
- **Take a band-aid only as a named interim.** Either form reaches a root
  worth fixing most of the time, and that fix is the default. Where the root
  is genuinely out of reach now, the patch ships labelled as a patch with the
  root recorded beside it, so the next reader meets a known interim instead
  of mistaking it for the rule.

## Boundary

Applies the moment a signal arrives and governs which root to chase and
whether a carve-out stands. It covers tracing one signal to its root while
the goal anchor holds. The reading of *accumulated* signal — a second human
correction or re-anchor on one goal in a thread, and the park / pivot /
bounce it calls for — belongs to the **attention-is-north-star** axiom's
drift rule; this file fires on the first signal, before any accumulation
reading is available. Whether a downstream overlay should exist at all is the
same earn-right one layer out, at the config surface, owned by
[`trust-upstream-defaults.md`](trust-upstream-defaults.md) — prove the
canonical default fails before authoring against it.
