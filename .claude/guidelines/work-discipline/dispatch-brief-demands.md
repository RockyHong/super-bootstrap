# Dispatch Brief Demands — Evidence the Returned Work Must Carry

A brief that names the outcome without naming the evidence gets the outcome
asserted back. The failures are silent by construction: a check authored
against already-conforming work emits the same pass whether or not it can
discriminate; an implementer free to choose its own readings after the fact
chooses the ones that moved the right way; a bound fitted to whatever single
sample was at hand rejects inputs it was never meant to exclude. Each returns
a report that reads correct. What follows governs whether a check can tell
pass from fail at all — where a gate sits in a pipeline, and whether its
verdict still binds the step after it, is a separate concern owned by the
device-channel principles on gate placement and feedback.

- **Demand a bite check on every assertion the work adds or rewrites** —
  feed an input that violates the assertion, watch the check fail while
  naming that input, remove the violation, watch it pass. The brief asks for
  the failure text verbatim in the report: a check that has never gone red
  and a working one are the same artifact on conforming input.
- **Name the readings that must not regress before the work starts** — any
  change carrying a measurable cost states them in the brief, and a reading
  moving the wrong way stops the work and returns the number for a decision.
  Keep the bound and the constant fixed while the reading is being read;
  re-tuning either to accommodate it conceals the quantity the change exists
  to price.
- **Calibrate a floor or tolerance on a measured spread** — sample across
  the input variation the bound must admit, place the bound outside the
  observed spread, and record the spread's width alongside the bound itself.
  A single measurement carries no width, so any margin drawn around it lands
  inside the real spread and starts rejecting legal inputs.
