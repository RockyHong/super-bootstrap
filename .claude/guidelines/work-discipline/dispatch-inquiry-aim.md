# Dispatch Inquiry Aim — The Question an Inquiry Brief Poses

A dispatch whose return informs a decision rather than executes a spec —
research, a survey, a market or segment map, an option scan — is an inquiry,
and its brief's question bounds what comes back: the receiver answers what it
was asked. A brief written as a checklist of facts gets the checklist filled
and its frame confirmed; a brief aimed at the decision gets a researcher that
weighs the evidence against it.

- **Name the decision the inquiry serves, and pose the question as that
  decision** — "is this segment worth entering, and in what form" rather
  than a list of figures to fetch. The decision is what the receiver aims its
  angles at and judges relevance against.
- **Carry the user's examples as samples, the user's limits as the
  boundary** — both ride verbatim ([`dispatch-breadcrumb.md`](dispatch-breadcrumb.md)).
  Words the user offered as instances ("like A or B") carry a gloss marking
  them as samples, and the receiver widens past them as far as the decision
  reaches; a limit the user stated ("only these three") binds as given.
- **Let the receiver derive the taxonomy** — ask it to establish the
  categories, the mainstream and the edges from the evidence before it
  collects, in place of a pre-cut category list to fill. The boundary is a
  finding the evidence draws. An inquiry split across parallel receivers
  derives the taxonomy once, in its own dispatch, and passes it to every
  member as a literal.
- **Ask for disconfirming evidence as hard as confirming** — name what would
  argue against the option and send the receiver after it with the same
  effort. An inquiry that searches only for support returns a rationale for
  the assumption it started from.
- **Ask back a map plus the receiver's read** — the landscape found
  (categories, leaders, movement, gaps), the categories it left out and why,
  the read it draws for the decision, and where the evidence runs thin.
  Figures ride inside the map, as its evidence.
