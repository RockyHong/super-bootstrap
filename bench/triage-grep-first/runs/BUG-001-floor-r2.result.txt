**Root cause:** at the end of `run()`, the engine closes out the period's statements before it posts the last held refund. `_emit_period()` sits at `engine.py:89`, one line before `_rollover(None)` at `:90`.

- Every refund waits one step in `_held`. A refund that isn't last gets posted when the next event arrives, which is why it works once moved up.
- A refund that is last gets posted by `_rollover(None)`. By then `_emit_period()` has already copied the statements out and emptied `_open`, so the refund lands on a fresh statement that is never returned. The closing balance stays 12.34 too high.
- The same code (`_apply_refund`) still records the refund in the replay ledger. So when support re-sent it as `R-88120-b`, the ledger correctly flagged it as a replay of `R-88120`, even though that refund never posted.

**Card's Prior set aside:** the `is_duplicate` theory only explains why `R-88120-b` was skipped, and that skip follows from the ledger entry above. It can't explain why `R-88120` itself was lost, or why its position in the batch matters. The ledger keys refunds by (account, amount, posted_on) on purpose, because processors don't echo the refund id back.
